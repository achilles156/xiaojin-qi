Research

My research explores how people have inhabited, shaped, and understood their environments, and how these ways of knowing have taken form in cities, landscapes, buildings, and material practices. Working across archaeology, urban and architectural history, historical geography, and heritage studies, I focus primarily on historical China.

My research begins with places themselves. Through fieldwork, I bring close observation of terrain, architecture, and material remains into dialogue with historical texts, maps, and images. I am particularly interested in the layered histories of places, and in how material evidence can illuminate the relationships between human experience, systems of knowledge, and historical change.

Research Interests

  • Historical Cities and Landscapes
  • Landscape, Perception, and Historical Knowledge
  • Material Knowledge and Spatial Practices
  • Archaeology and Heritage Interpretation
